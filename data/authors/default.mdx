I study how AI-generated suggestions shape how people see themselves and present themselves to others. I build generative AI prototypes and use interviews to learn how people respond to them. I am applying to PhD programs in human–computer interaction for fall 2027.

## Research

My work so far follows one thread. In [Beyond Adaptation](/publications/beyond-adaptation), seven formative interviews suggested that people who want to connect across cultures often hold back, afraid of saying the wrong thing. I built an LLM-supported prototype that offers self-presentation ideas as people prepare to meet others. It was published as a [CHI EA 2025 Late-Breaking Work](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706599.3719929).

My master’s thesis turned to the other side of such suggestions: when people ask generative AI to help present them, it also offers readings of who they are. I built a system that turns personal material into tags, narratives, and images of the self, and studied it in think-aloud sessions with twenty emerging adults. [The paper](/publications/self-interpretations) is under review.

I am now conducting the interviews and analysis for a study in Prof. Bi’s lab on how people wait for AI agents to finish delegated tasks.

Next, I want to ask whether people still have room to judge what AI suggests about them, and how suggestions can be designed to keep that room open: easy to decline, and clear about where they came from.

My earlier research includes experiments with 15 participants on a perspective-switching projection system in VR, advised by Lung-Pan Cheng, and a rider arrival-rate prediction model for Global Express, advised by Ling-Chieh Kung.
